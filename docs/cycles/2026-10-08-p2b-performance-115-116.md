## WHAT WAS IMPLEMENTED
- **Set-based lifecycle writer** (`monitoring/diff.py`, commit
  `6dc62d3`): 5 constant statements per cycle (was 3 + N);
  transitions share one UPDATE with a VALUES list, continuing
  identities carry their prior (state, stamp) via one more.
  `tests/test_finding_lifecycle.py` passes byte-unmodified;
  `tests/test_lifecycle_query_count.py` pins 5 statements at 45
  and at 90 identities.
- **Benchmark suite** (`tools/bench.py`) + `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`:
  labeled local measurements (migrations 0.28 s; anon scan
  1.83 ms mean; action center — the heaviest read — 41.25 ms
  mean; worker 7.27 jobs/s on mocks; lifecycle 12 ms@10 →
  201 ms@500 identities).
- **Batched notifications + the inbox rule** (commit `1387fc4`):
  `notify.create_notifications_batch` (one connection, one
  advisory lock, one dedupe SELECT, one INSERT); per-finding
  notices are in-app only; email is the once-per-cycle
  scan_summary digest; the per-item loop remains as the
  failure fallback.
- Consent copy corrected in `static/app.js` and
  `static/index.html` to the true digest behavior.

## WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT IMPLEMENTED
A connection pool / persistent connections: the residual ~10 s
hook latency is connection churn over the WAN link, recorded as
the architecture's known scaling tax (breaks-first item 2) —
changing the pool model is a platform decision, not a patch.
