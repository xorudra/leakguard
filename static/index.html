      Trust & security

      Exactly what LeakGuard stores, what it never stores, who else touches your data, and how long anything is kept. Every claim on this page describes the code that is actually running — nothing here is aspirational.

      What we store — and how

      • Your saved details (emails, phones, names, addresses, usernames, domains) are encrypted with AES-256-GCM envelope encryption: every record gets its own fresh random encryption key, and that key is itself encrypted under a master key that lives only in the server's environment — never in the database, never in the code.
      • Your account email is looked up by a keyed one-way fingerprint (HMAC), stored encrypted the same way as your saved details, and only ever displayed masked (like r•••@example.com). It is never stored in plain text.
      • Scan results and removal cases are stored against your account so monitoring and the timeline work. The anonymous Quick Scan stores nothing at all — its results are computed per request and forgotten.

      What we never store

      • Passwords. Account passwords exist only as Argon2id password hashes — we could not read yours back if we wanted to.
      • Sign-in sessions and API tokens. Only their SHA-256 hashes are stored, so a database copy alone contains nothing anyone could sign in with.
      • Passwords you check for breaches. The check uses k-anonymity: only the first 5 characters of the password's SHA-1 hash are sent to Have I Been Pwned, the match is made locally, and the password itself is never sent, stored or logged.
      • Payment details. LeakGuard is free and has no payments — we hold no card or bank information about anyone.

      Who else processes data

      • Render — hosts the app. Neon — hosts the database (which holds only the encrypted forms described above).
      • Brevo — sends account email (password resets, and monitoring alerts if you switch notifications on). It sees the recipient address and the message.
      • XposedOrNot and Have I Been Pwned — the breach databases your email is checked against; checking means they receive the address being checked, the same as if you used their own sites.
      • DuckDuckGo — public-web discovery searches. Domain checks read public DNS via Cloudflare and public certificate records via crt.sh. Username checks read the public profile pages of the platforms themselves.

      There is no advertising, no analytics tracking, and no selling or sharing of data with anyone beyond the processors above.

      Where data lives

      • The app runs on Render in Oregon, United States.
      • The database is hosted by Neon in AWS ap-southeast-1 (Singapore). It stores the encrypted and hashed forms described above.
      • Email and scan sources receive only the specific data each request needs, as described in “Who else processes data” and in the full privacy policy. LeakGuard does not choose a different region per user; these are fixed locations for the current free services.

      How long things are kept

      • Sessions: deleted 7 days after they expire or are revoked.
      • Password-reset tokens: deleted 7 days after they are used or expire.
      • Notifications: 90 days.
      • A deleted account: everything it owns is hard-deleted 30 days after deletion — saved details, scans, findings, removal cases, settings, household and tokens. A count-only audit trail (actions and numbers, never values or personal data) is kept.

      The honest limits of removal

      • Can be removed: data brokers, people-search sites, and Google search results — they must answer a legal erasure request, and a removal only ever counts as done here after LeakGuard has gone back and verified the listing is gone.
      • Cannot be removed: a breach dump already copied to Telegram, dark-web forums or torrents. No tool can delete every copy — anyone promising that is lying.

      Your rights, in practice

      • See everything: Privacy Center → “Download my data” exports all of it as one file (JSON or CSV).
      • Withdraw permission: every consent switch in the Privacy Center takes effect instantly and is recorded.
      • Delete everything: Privacy Center → “Delete account”. You are signed out immediately and your data becomes inaccessible; the 30-day window above exists so an accidental or malicious deletion can still be undone by support, then the hard delete runs.
      • Extra protection: optional two-factor authentication (authenticator app) and optional passkeys (sign in with your device's fingerprint, face or PIN — LeakGuard stores only the passkey's public half) are available in the Privacy Center.

      Privacy policy

      Effective date: 7 October 2026. This policy describes what the LeakGuard code running on this site actually does. It claims no certification or formal compliance status.

      Information stored for an account

      • Account email: stored as a keyed HMAC lookup value, an AES-256-GCM encrypted value, and a masked display form — not in plain text.
      • Saved details: emails, phone numbers, names, addresses, usernames and domains you save are stored the same way: encrypted, with an HMAC lookup value and a masked display form.
      • Passwords and sign-in: account passwords are stored only as Argon2id hashes. Session, reset and API tokens are stored only as SHA-256 hashes. Two-factor secrets are encrypted. For a passkey, only its public key is stored.
      • Scan and removal records: findings record the provider, source, exposed field names, confidence and an evidence hash — not the raw saved detail. Removal cases record the broker, status and evidence. If a removal letter is generated, it necessarily contains the details you supplied for that letter and is shown only in your account.
      • Settings, permissions and notifications: your consent history, monitoring choices and in-app notifications are stored so those features work.

      What is not stored in logs

      Passwords are never stored in plain text. Application and audit records are built not to contain plaintext saved details, passwords, sign-in tokens or vault keys. The audit trail uses actions, identifiers for records inside the system, and counts; it contains no email or saved-detail values.

      Anonymous Quick Scan

      The Quick Scan needs no account and creates no server-side scan record. The email is processed during that request and the result is returned to your browser; it is not saved as an account scan. If you also check a password, only the first five characters of its SHA-1 hash are sent to Have I Been Pwned and the match is made by LeakGuard. Your browser keeps a small last-scan summary (the email, breach count, score and date) in its own local storage on your device so it can show a change next time; that summary is not a server-side LeakGuard record.

      Retention, export and deletion

      • Saved details stay until you remove them or delete the account.
